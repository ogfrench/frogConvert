# Visual and copy anti-patterns to review, not prohibit

The signs below are an unreliable test for AI authorship. Apply them as
questions about **purpose, proportion, accessibility and repetition**.

## Twenty common visual cues

1. **Tags and pills:** statuses or filters, or badges for everything?
2. **Excessive italics:** earned emphasis, or the same twist every screen?
3. **Instrument Serif:** an appropriate specified font, or fashionable display type?
4. **Negative kerning:** does tight tracking actually remain legible?
5. **Over-detailed UI:** essential workflow, or fake polished complexity?
6. **Overusing dividers:** meaningful groups, or lines instead of spacing?
7. **Color shades and opacities:** semantic hierarchy, or arbitrary variants?
8. **Containers with borders:** actual components or every idea in a box?
9. **Overly detailed diagrams:** clear mechanism, or ornamental complexity?
10. **Verbose, redundant text:** evidence and caveats, or paraphrased titles?
11. **Code breadcrumbs:** real developer context, or decorative terminal labels?
12. **Section numbering:** required navigation and order, or pseudo-rigor?
13. **Eyebrows:** orientation beyond the title, or duplicated section labels?
14. **Dithering:** intentional brand texture, or a default surface effect?
15. **ASCII images:** useful technical explanation, or nostalgic decoration?
16. **Squircles:** appropriate component geometry, or a fashionable default?
17. **Excessive mono fonts:** actual identifiers/data, or style for everything?
18. **ALL CAPS subtitles:** legible and prescribed, or performative emphasis?
19. **Gradient backgrounds:** actual identity or depth, or empty spectacle?
20. **Periods in titles:** follow context and editorial conventions, not an AI rule.

## Additional visual signals

- Repeated eyebrow, headline, subtitle, three-card section on every screen
- Bento layouts, rounded cards and icons used regardless of content relationship
- Arbitrary dashboard metrics, progress rings, faux product screenshots
- Generic stock photos pretending to depict actual users or evidence
- Overuse of glow, grain, gradients, glass and motion without a reason
- Minimalism that removes critical data, or tiny annotations that hide its meaning
- The same italicized word, marked phrase and dramatic contrast on every slide
- Charts without units, baselines, uncertainty or comparable series
- A visual metaphor that replaces rather than explains a genuine mechanism

## Copy and reasoning signals

- "Unlock", "elevate", "empower", "seamless", "game-changing", "transform"
- Unnamed authority such as "research proves", "leaders agree", "industry standard"
- Repeated "not X but Y", "from X to Y", dramatic fragments and forced triads
- Abstract subjects: "innovation", "solutions", "the future" doing magic
- Fabricated numbers, unsourced direct quotes, fake case studies or unwarranted precision
- Claiming that a pilot, observation or correlation proves a causal universal result
- Reporting activity as outcome: workshops and frameworks in place of findings
- A subtitle restating the headline; verbose text surrounding an already clear chart
- Premature promises, motivational closings and a vague "Let's align" call to action
- Empty empathy or audience assumptions without research
- Overuse of punctuation, all caps, bold/italic or ornamental labels

Good alternatives: name an actual actor and observed change; show a fair
comparison with units and source; label inference as inference; show uncertainty;
specify a decision, owner and consequence. If evidence is absent, say so.
