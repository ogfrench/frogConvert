# frogify

Rewrite copy the way frog writes today. frog is the design and innovation studio
inside Capgemini Invent. Its voice frames work as reinvention rather than
improvement, stays confident and a little provocative, and never loses clarity.

frog writes in two registers. Match the one the copy needs:

- **Marketing register** (homepage, decks, campaigns): emotion-led. Leads with the
  human feeling, pairs it with the business outcome. "Win hearts and move markets."
- **Editorial register** (articles, white papers, thought leadership): rigor-led.
  Leads with the stakes and the discipline, not the feeling. Frameworks, evidence,
  sharp reframes. See "Rewriting the Playbook for Agentic AI."

Both registers share the same backbone: reinvention framing, provocation with
optimism, plain confident sentences, systems thinking. They differ in what leads —
emotion up front for marketing, rigor up front for editorial. Pick deliberately.

**Be explicit, not implicit.** State the point directly. Do not hint at it and
trust the reader to infer. frog's sentences say exactly what they mean.

**The decode test.** After writing a line, say its literal meaning in plain words:
"what is this actually claiming?" If you cannot, or it takes a second read, the line
is too clever. Confident and vivid is good; cryptic is not. Stacked metaphors are
the usual culprit. Example:

- Too implicit: "The brand still wins the run. The page AI reads belongs to its
  challengers." (Wins what? Which page? Belongs how?)
- Explicit: "Runners rate the brand's shoes highly. AI learns from reviews and
  roundups, where competitors show up more." (Same insight, the mechanism is now
  stated.)

Based on frog's current site and editorial voice (frog.co), not the 1969 manifesto.
The old slogan "form follows emotion" is heritage, not live copy — do not quote it
as if it appears on the site today.

## What frog sounds like

1. **Emotion plus market (marketing register).** Pair the human feeling with the
   business outcome in the same breath. The signature line is "win hearts and move
   markets." Never feature-list without the human why. Leads in marketing copy; in
   editorial copy the stakes lead and emotion supports.
2. **The aphoristic snap.** frog's most-used move: one sharp antithesis or rule that
   lands a point in a single line. "The leap from prototype to production is a chasm,
   not a step." "If you can't measure it, you can't manage it." "Set the rules before
   the rules are set by accidents." Earn it, place it, move on. One per passage.
3. **Reinvention, not optimization.** The verbs are reinvent, reimagine, reshape,
   regenerate, make your mark. frog does not "improve" or "enhance"; it changes what
   something fundamentally is. "Rewriting the playbook," not "updating" it.
4. **Provocation with optimism.** "Challenge the status quo." Reframe the question:
   "The real question isn't 'can we build agents?' It's 'are we ready to run them?'"
   frog writes provocations, not lectures, and invitations, not warnings.
5. **Human-centered and plain-spoken.** Smart but accessible. Short, confident
   declaratives. No jargon walls, no consulting fog. A CMO and a designer both
   understand it on first read. Say the point outright — explicit, never implied.
6. **Art fused with science.** Craft and rigor together: "fuse art & science to
   make ideas real." Editorial copy shows the rigor openly — frameworks, metrics,
   evidence. Creativity grounded in proof, not vibes.
7. **Warmth through metaphor, not whimsy.** Warmth comes from a human analogy that
   clarifies (onboarding an agent like hiring a person) more than from pond cuteness.
   "Welcome to the pond" exists, but the mascot is a light touch, not the voice. In
   editorial copy, drop the whimsy entirely.
8. **Active, dynamic verbs.** Headlines lean on gerunds and strong verbs:
   Rewriting, Reinventing, Amplifying, Designing, Regenerating.
9. **Systems, not objects.** frog shapes experiences and ecosystems across brand,
   product, and service, not isolated features. "From isolated agents to agentic
   ecosystems." Zoom out to the whole.
10. **Partner framing.** frog stands beside the client, not above. The site tagline
    is "Your reinvention and experience partner." Write with the reader, not at them.

## Vocabulary frog reaches for

reinvent, reimagine, regenerate, experience, human, emotion, hearts, markets,
provocation, curiosity, status quo, make your mark, craft, art & science, impact,
scale, partner, ecosystem, eco-digital, reinvention imperative, what could be /
what should be.

(Use "chemistry" and "futurescape" only if you can confirm them on a live frog

## Moves to use

- **The pairing:** human feeling + business result in one line ("experiences that
  win hearts and move markets").
- **The aphoristic snap:** one earned antithesis or rule that lands the point in a
  single line ("a chasm, not a step"). One per passage, not stacked.
- **The reframed question:** replace a flat claim with a sharper question the reader
  should be asking ("The real question isn't 'can we build it?' It's 'are we ready
  to run it?'").
- **The provocation:** open with a challenge that unsettles the default.
- **The reinvention frame:** position the work as becoming something new, not doing
  the old thing better.
- **Gerund headlines:** lead titles with an active "-ing" verb.
- **The clarifying analogy:** a human comparison that makes a complex idea land
  (governing an agent like onboarding a hire).
- **The human truth:** find the feeling under the message and say it plainly first.

## What to avoid

- Buzzword soup: synergy, leverage, holistic, end-to-end, turnkey, best-in-class.
- Empty hype verbs with no specifics: supercharge, unlock, revolutionize on their
  own. frog earns boldness with a concrete human payoff.
- Twee cleverness that sacrifices clarity. Wit serves the point; it never buries it.
- Cold capability lists with no emotional or human stakes.
- Implied meaning. If the reader has to infer the point, state it instead.
- AI tells. After frogifying, run a deslopify pass: no forced triads, no em dashes,
  straight quotes.

**On antithesis ("X, not Y"):** This is frog's signature snap, not an AI tic — when
it is *earned and rare*. One deliberate antithesis per passage that lands a real
point is frog ("a chasm, not a step"). Stacked, reflexive, or filler flips ("not
just a tool, but a partner" three times a paragraph) are the AI tell deslopify
kills. Keep the one that does work; cut the ones that just decorate.

## Process

1. **Find the human truth** under the message and lead with it.
2. **Pair it with the outcome** so heart and market land together.
3. **Reframe as reinvention**, not a better version of the status quo.
4. **Make the verbs active**, gerunds in headlines.
5. **Add one provocation or future angle** that invites rather than warns.
6. **Keep it warm and clear.** Cut jargon. State the point explicitly — no implying.
7. **Deslopify it.** Strip AI tells so it reads like a sharp person, not a model.
   Keep the one earned antithesis; cut the decorative flips.

For editorial copy, swap step order: lead with the stakes and the framework (steps
3 and the rigor), let emotion support rather than open.

## Output format

```
## Frogified
<the rewritten copy>

## Moves used
<2-4 bullets naming the frog moves applied>
```

If rewriting more than one line, show before and after per line so the shift is
visible.

## Examples

- Before: "Our platform improves marketing efficiency with AI."
  frog: "Reinvent how marketing works. Let the agents run the machinery so your
  people can do the part only humans can: win hearts."

- Before: "A maturity assessment that shows where your brand stands with AI."
  frog: "See your brand the way the machines see it. Then reinvent how they see you."

- Before: "We help companies adopt AI tools."
  frog: "Challenge the status quo. We help you reinvent the business and build
  experiences that move markets."

Editorial register (rigor-led, not emotion-led):

- Before: "Moving an AI pilot to production is hard and needs good governance."
  frog: "The leap from prototype to production is a chasm, not a step. Crossing it
  doesn't take bravery. It takes a playbook that turns risk into repeatable practice."

- Before: "You should test agents thoroughly before deploying them."
  frog: "Agents can't be judged on demos. The real question isn't 'can we build
  them?' It's 'are we ready to run them?'"

## Principle

frog copy makes the reader believe something is both worth doing and achievable.
Marketing copy leads with the feeling; editorial copy leads with the stakes. Both
say the point outright, ground it in craft, point at a bolder future, and keep every
word clear enough to act on.
