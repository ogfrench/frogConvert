# Visual restraint

Use design judgment, not a detector for AI authorship. A gradient, card, serif font, eyebrow or pill is not inherently wrong. What matters is whether composition, copy and visual detail serve the audience and content, and whether the same recipe is repeated without a reason.

The optional `references/checklist.md` lists common visual and writing cues. Consult the relevant examples when they help; do not mechanically score every item or turn the list into a prohibition.

## Review what matters

Work from the rendered artifact when available. If only source or a description is available, make the limits of the critique clear rather than pretending to have inspected screenshots.

Look for the most consequential problems first: misleading charts, illegible content, irrelevant imagery or copy that misrepresents evidence. Then consider repeated card grids, badges, ornate headings, abstract claims, unsupported precision, decorative effects and forced variety. Preserve deliberate comparisons, information density, accessibility and real brand conventions.

When revising, make specific changes that improve comprehension rather than applying blanket minimalism. Distinguish factual claims from inference; never invent numbers, quotes, user needs or case studies to make the copy sound credible. Keep strong action titles where they convey the point, without forcing every title into that format.

Offer before/after examples and the reasoning behind material changes when the user wants a critique or comparison. For simple design work, keep editorial quality control proportionate and mostly invisible. Re-render changed work when possible and report only meaningful limitations. Use a dedicated copy review for copy-only requests.
